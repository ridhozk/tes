 1. Background
     Operational activities within the Corridor Asset are executed in an increasingly complex environment,
     involving multiple disciplines, facilities, contractors, and simultaneous operations. In daily execution,
     challenges often arise not due to lack of effort or commitment, but as a result of late changes,
     unplanned activities, resource overload, and misalignment between planning assumptions and actual
     field conditions. These situations frequently lead to rework, standby, increased safety exposure,
     schedule disruption, and elevated operational pressure at field level.

     Historically, short-term driven decisions or fragmented planning approaches have proven insufficient
     to manage such complexity in a sustainable manner. Activities introduced too late in the planning
     cycle tend to bypass proper readiness checks, create conflicts with ongoing operations, and force
     execution teams into reactive modes. This not only impacts productivity and cost but also erodes
     operational stability and execution discipline.

     To address these challenges, an integrated planning approach is required—one that progressively
     screens, validates, and prepares activities before execution. Integrated planning ensures that work
     is not reacted to at the last moment but instead managed through a structured hierarchy of planning
     horizons with increasing levels of detail, readiness, and execution control.

     The 12-Month Plan serves as the primary entry point of this integrated planning system. It provides
     early visibility of anticipated activities, workload distribution, and potential constraints, enabling early
     identification of risks, capacity limitations, interdependencies, and execution challenges. The 12-
     Month Plan is not intended as an execution commitment; rather, it functions as a screening and
     alignment mechanism to ensure that only activities that are relevant, feasible, and strategically
     aligned progress into more detailed planning stages.

     Through subsequent planning horizons—90-Day, 30-Day, and 7-Day Plans—activities are
     progressively refined, validated, and prepared for execution. Each planning stage increases the level
     of certainty and control, ensuring that execution is supported by confirmed scope, resources, material
     readiness, risk mitigation, and coordination across disciplines.

     By establishing a structured planning hierarchy starting from the 12-Month Plan, this procedure aims
     to protect operational teams from unprepared work, excessive last-minute changes, and uncontrolled
     execution pressure. At the same time, it maintains flexibility through governed change and break-in
     processes, allowing the organization to respond to legitimate operational needs without
     compromising safety, stability, or planning discipline.

     workload screening through medium term planning to daily execution across the Corridor Operation

 3. Scope
     This procedure applies to the end to end planning and execution control of all operational and
     operationally impacting activities across the Corridor Operation Asset.

     It governs all activities proposed for execution within an integrated planning horizon, commencing
     from early workload screening through the 12 Month Plan, and continuing through the rolling 90
     Day (90D), 30 Day (30D), and 7 Day (7D) planning cycles, covering workload identification,
     prioritization, readiness validation, execution commitment (Frozen), and weekly execution control.

     This procedure applies to all activities that may impact operational safety, asset integrity, production
     stability, or execution capacity, including but not limited to:

         •   Operations and Maintenance (Preventive and Corrective Maintenance),
         •   Reliability and Asset Integrity activities,
         •   Projects, modifications, and Management of Change (MOC),
         •   Offsite, Marine, and logistics related activities,
         •   Drilling and well related activities,
         •   Other internal or external activities that interface with or influence ongoing operations.

     This procedure is applicable to all Company and Contractor personnel involved in planning,
     coordination, approval, and execution governance, including but not limited to Function Planners,
     Asset Activity Coordinators, Job Owners, Supervisors, and Operations Leadership.

     This procedure establishes the sole authorized medium term and short term planning and execution
     control framework for Corridor operations and shall be applied as a single, integrated system.

     It does not authorize execution of activities outside approved Frozen Plans, nor does it replace or
     override applicable HSE, Permit-to-Work, Management of Change, or technical procedures, all of
     which shall continue to apply in accordance with their respective approval gates.

 5. Objectives
     The Integrated Planning and Execution framework operates as a single, sequential governance
     chain across the 12-Month, 90-Day, 30-Day, and 7-Day planning horizons, with each horizon
     serving a distinct objective and control function.

     12 Month Planning (12M)

     The objective of the 12-Month Plan (12M) is to establish a consolidated, risk-informed, and
     capacity-balanced medium-term planning baseline that enables safe, reliable, and efficient
     operations.

     Specifically, the 12 Month Plan aims to:

          •   Provide a single, integrated view of all operational and operationally impacting activities
              planned or anticipated within the next twelve (12) months, including major milestones,
              shutdowns, maintenance programs, projects, rig moves, engine overhaul programs,
              pipeline and roadwork, support services, and key commercial or contractual requirements.
          •   Ensure early identification and protection of mandatory activities, including safety-critical,
              asset integrity, regulatory, and license-to-operate requirements.
          •   Screen and prioritize activities based on risk exposure, business impact, asset criticality,
              and execution complexity.
          •   Enable early visibility of capacity constraints, major interfaces, and resource demands to
              prevent future execution overload.
          •   Improve overall plan stability by reducing late changes, unplanned work, and reactive
              execution pressure.
          •   Provide a credible and structured input to the 90 Day Integrated Planning process without
              constituting execution commitment.
          •   Support proactive risk mitigation, resource planning, and management decision making
              across the Corridor Asset.

     90 Day Planning (90D)

     The objective of the 90 Day Plan is to provide a forward-looking, integrated planning outlook that
     identifies, consolidates, and prioritizes all anticipated operational and supporting activities within the
     next ninety (90) days.

     The 90 Day Plan enables:

          •   Early visibility and control of upcoming operational workload and competing demands.

           •   Prevention of over-commitment of resources by balancing workload against available
               execution capacity.
           •   Early identification of critical risks, high-value activities, major interfaces, SIMOPS
               exposure, and readiness challenges.
           •   Alignment between operational objectives, maintenance strategy, project activities, and
               business priorities.
           •   Provision of an integrated and risk-informed basis for management decision-making.

     The 90 Day Plan serves as the primary strategic feeder to the 30 Day Plan and authorizes activities

     30 Day Planning (30D)

     The objective of the 30-Day Plan is to translate the approved 90-Day outlook into a validated,
     prioritized, and execution-ready plan for the upcoming thirty (30) days.

     The 30 Day Plan ensures that:

           •   Activities are clearly defined, appropriately scoped, and correctly prioritized.
           •   Key readiness elements including manpower, materials, equipment, permits, interfaces,
               SIMOPS controls, and HSE requirements are identified, tracked, and validated.
           •   Cross functional alignment is achieved across Operations, Maintenance, Projects, Offsite,
               and supporting functions prior to execution commitment.
           •   Execution risks and constraints are understood and managed before work is frozen.

     The 30 Day Plan represents a controlled execution commitment, subject to readiness verification
     and formal Gate 2 governance approval, after which activities are declared Frozen

     7-Day Planning (7D)

     The objective of the 7-Day Plan is to establish a final, executable weekly work plan that ensures
     activities are fully ready, authorized, fixed-scheduled, and safe to execute.

     The 7-Day Plan ensures that:

           •   Only ready and verified frozen activities are released for execution.
           •   Daily execution sequencing (fixed schedule), site coordination, and interface management
               are clearly defined.
           •   Short-term risks, constraints, SIMOPS exposure, and emerging changes are actively
               monitored and managed.

     The 7-Day Plan represents the final execution authorization. Any deviation from the approved plan,
     including scope, sequencing, or activity changes, shall be managed through the formal break-in or
     change control processes in accordance with this procedure

 6. Basic Principle
     6.1       Integrated and Single Planning System

     All planning, execution, monitoring, and improvement activities shall operate as one integrated and
     unified management system. No parallel planning, informal commitments, or execution outside the
     approved planning framework is permitted. All activities shall follow the defined planning horizons,
     approval gates, and control mechanisms established in this procedure

     6.2     Risk and Priority Based Planning and Execution

     Activities shall be planned, prioritized, and executed based on risk exposure, safety and regulatory
     obligations, asset integrity, and operational impact, rather than urgency alone. Mandatory activities
     related to safety, integrity, and compliance shall always take precedence over discretionary or
     deferrable work.

     6.3     Progressive Planning and Execution Discipline

     Planning shall follow a progressive maturity approach across the 12-Month, 90-Day, 30-Day, and 7-
     Day planning horizons. Execution commitment shall only occur after activities have passed the
     required readiness validation and formal approval gates. Frozen plans shall not be changed without
     approved break-in authorization.

     6.4 Cross-Functional Collaboration and Alignment

     Effective planning and execution require collaboration and alignment across Operations,
     Maintenance, Projects, Offsite, and supporting functions. Activities shall not be planned, committed,
     or executed in isolation. Cross-functional interfaces, SIMOPS exposure, and resource conflicts shall
     be identified and resolved prior to execution commitment.

     6.5     Safety, Compliance, and Authority

     Safety, regulatory compliance, and asset integrity shall always take precedence over schedule,
     cost, or productivity targets. Stop Work Authority remains valid at all times, and no planning
     approval or execution commitment overrides safety judgment, permit to work requirements, or
     Management of Change (MOC) controls.

     6.6     Performance Monitoring and Continuous Improvement

     Execution performance shall be monitored using defined planning and execution indicators,
     including Plan Attainment, Plan Stability, and Break-in frequency. Performance data and lessons
     learned shall be used to improve planning quality, execution discipline, and system effectiveness,
     rather mandatory than for individual blame or punitive action.

 7. Planning Process
     The integrated planning process ensures that all operational and operationally impacting activities
     progress through a structured, readiness-based lifecycle prior to execution.
     This process governs the transition from early workload screening and medium-term outlook to
     execution commitment and final field delivery.Planning shall be managed through sequential activity
     identification, classification, and readiness assessment across defined planning horizons, ensuring
     that only prepared, approved, and capacity-balanced activities proceed to execution.

     7.1   12 Month Planning Process (12M)
     7.1.1 Activity Classification

             Activities shall be classified into the following categories.

           a. Mandatory Activities

              Activities that shall be executed to maintain safety, asset integrity, regulatory compliance,
              and license to operate.

              Mandatory activities typically include, but are not limited to:

               •    Safety-Critical Element (SCE) inspections, testing, maintenance, and remediation
               •    Asset integrity management requirements (e.g., corrosion control, integrity repairs)
               •    Statutory, regulatory, certification, and contractual obligations
               •    Corrective actions arising from incidents, audits, regulatory findings, or risk

               •    Maintenance or operational activities required to prevent loss of containment, major

           b. Value Adding Activities

              Activities that generate operational, reliability, or business improvements but are not
              immediately required to maintain safety, integrity, or regulatory compliance.

              Value-Adding activities typically include, but are not limited to:

               •    Reliability improvement initiatives and proactive maintenance enhancements
               •    Production optimization, debottlenecking, and efficiency improvements
               •    Preventive maintenance beyond minimum compliance requirements
               •    Projects or modifications that improve operability, maintainability, or lifecycle cost
               •    Technology upgrades and system enhancements

              Value-Adding activities shall be prioritized based on risk reduction, business value,
              strategic alignment, and available execution capacity, and may be deferred when
              constrained by higher priority mandatory work

     7.1.2 Readiness (Early Screening)

             At the 12 Month Planning stage, readiness assessment shall be conducted at a high-level
             screening to identify major constraints, risks, and feasibility concerns. Readiness at this
             stage is indicative only and shall not be interpreted as execution readiness or authorization.

     7.2     90 Day Planning Process (90D)

     The 90-Day Planning Process provides a forward-looking, integrated planning outlook that
     consolidates and prioritizes anticipated operational and supporting activities.

     7.2.1 Activity Classification

             At this stage, activities shall be categorized to determine eligibility for progression toward
             execution readiness:

              •    Mandatory – Essential for safety, integrity, regulatory, or compliance requirements
              •    Value-Adding – Improve production, efficiency, or reliability
              •    Highlight business event- is a key activity or event that impact business outcomes.
              •    Deferrable – Can be safely postponed without immediate operational risk
              •    Break-In – Activities proposed for inclusion after plan finalization and during the gate
                   approval period that were not originally scheduled

     7.2.2 Readiness (Outlook)

           High-level readiness shall be assessed for major activities, including:

            •   Clear scope and methodology
            •   Engineering and technical maturity
            •   Material and equipment availability
            •   Contracting and manpower readiness

            •   Permit, regulatory, and external dependency considerations

 8. Activity Entry and Screening Criteria
     This section defines the minimum criteria and control rules governing how activities are proposed,
     screened, and accepted into the integrated planning system, ensuring planning remains structured,
     risk-based, and capacity-balanced

     8.1      General Principles

     •     All operational and operationally impacting activities shall enter the planning system through the
           defined planning horizons and shall not bypass the integrated planning process.
     •     Activities shall not be committed for execution unless they have been screened, prioritized, and
           approved in accordance with this procedure.
     •     Urgency alone shall not justify activity inclusion or execution without proper screening and
           approval.

     8.2      Activity Entry into the 12 Month Plan (12M)

     An activity may be proposed for inclusion in the 12-Month Plan if it meets the following minimum
     criteria:
     • The activity objective and intent are clearly defined at a conceptual level.
     • The activity has a potential impact on safety, asset integrity, regulatory compliance, production,
          or operational capacity; or
     • The activity represents a known or anticipated requirement (e.g., maintenance program,
          project, inspection, regulatory obligation, campaign, or support service).

     Activities entering the 12-Month Plan shall be subject to early screening only and shall not be
     considered execution-ready or execution-committed

     8.3      Activity Entry into the 90 Day Plan (90D)

     An activity may progress from the 12-Month Plan into the 90-Day Plan if:
     • The activity has been identified and screened in the 12-Month Plan; or
     • The activity is newly identified but has received approval to enter the planning system due to
         emerging operational needs.

     At the 90-Day stage, activities shall:
     • Be classified as Mandatory, Value-Adding, Deferrable, or Break-In.
     • Undergo high-level readiness outlook and risk screening.
     • Be evaluated for execution feasibility, resource implications, and interface exposure.

     Activities with significant unresolved feasibility issues shall not progress to the 30-Day Plan without
     an agreed mitigation path.

     8.4      Activity Entry into the 30-Day Plan (30D)

     An activity may be included in the 30-Day Plan if it meets one of the following conditions:
     • It has progressed through the 12-Month and 90-Day planning processes and is identified as a
         candidate for execution commitment; or
     • It is a routine or BAU activity included as a visibility item only, without execution commitment.

     Activities proposed for execution commitment in the 30-Day Plan shall:
     • Have defined scope and execution intent.

     •     Be assessed for readiness, resource availability, and cross-functional impact.
     •     Be subject to Gate 2 approval and freeze discipline.

     Activities that do not meet execution commitment criteria shall be included as visibility only items or

     8.5      Activity Entry into the 7 Day Plan (7D)

     Only activities that meet the following criteria may be included in the 7-Day Plan:
     • Activities declared Frozen through the approved 30-Day Plan; or
     • Routine and Business as usual activities approved for weekly execution under normal
         operational controls.

     No new scope, additional activities, or method changes shall be introduced at the 7-Day stage
     without an approved break-in or change control process.

     8.6      Break-In Activity Entry

     Break-in activities may be proposed only when:
     • There is a clear and demonstrable safety, integrity, regulatory, or operational necessity; or
     • Deferral would introduce unacceptable risk or loss of operational control.

     All break-in activities shall be:
     • Assessed for risk, readiness, and capacity impact.
     • Formally approved at the appropriate authority level in accordance with this procedure.
     • Tracked and monitored as part of execution performance review.

11. Break in Management and Change Control
     This section defines the governance, criteria, and control mechanisms for managing any changes
     to approved plans, ensuring that planning discipline is maintained while allowing controlled
     response to legitimate operational needs

     11.1   Principle of Break in Control

     All approved plans, particularly the 90Day Plan (Prioritize) and 30Day Plan (Frozen) shall be treated
     as controlled commitments. No addition, removal, or modification of activities shall be made after
     approval unless processed through a formal break-in or change control mechanism. Break in shall
     be considered an exception, not routine practice.

     11.2   Definition of Break in

     A break in is defined as:
     • Addition of new activity not originally planned
     • Removal or deferral of approved activity
     • Change in scope, method, sequence, or execution window
     • Any modification that impacts safety, resources, interfaces, or execution outcome

     11.3   Break-in Justification Criteria

     Break-in shall only be allowed under the following conditions:
     • Safety or integrity risk that cannot be deferred
     • Regulatory or compliance requirement requiring immediate action
     • Operational necessity to prevent production loss or major disruption
     • Unforeseen failure or emergent condition not previously identified

     Break in shall not be justified solely by urgency, convenience, or late planning

     11.4   Break in Approval Authority

     Break-in approval shall follow the applicable governance level based on impact:
     • Within 90-Day Plan (Prioritize Activities):
        Approval by VP or delegated.
     • Within 30-Day Plan (Frozen scope):
        Approval by OIM (plant) or OM (offsite)
     • High-impact or cross-functional break-in:
        May require escalation to VP or relevant management authority

     11.5   Break in Assessment Requirements

     All break-in requests shall be supported by:
     • Clear justification and urgency rationale
     • Risk assessment, including HSE and operational impact
     • Impact analysis on existing frozen plans (resource, schedule, SIMOPS, etc.)
     • Confirmation of readiness level for execution
     • Proposed mitigation for any disruption caused to existing commitments

     11.6   Break-in Implementation Control

     Approved break-in activities shall:
     • Be formally recorded and communicated to all relevant stakeholders
     • Be integrated into the current plan with updated sequencing and coordination
     • Not compromise safety, permit requirements, or execution control standards

     Rejected break in requests shall not be executed under any circumstances

     11.7   Break-in Monitoring and Performance Control

     All break-in activities shall be:
     • Tracked and reported as part of planning and execution performance
     • Reviewed periodically to identify trends, root causes, and improvement opportunities

     Excessive break-in frequency shall be treated as an indicator of:
     • Poor planning quality
     • Inadequate screening or readiness
     • Weak execution discipline

     Corrective actions should be implemented accordingly.

     11.8   Relationship with Change Control (MOC and Technical Changes)

     Break-in governance does not replace formal Management of Change (MOC) or technical approval
     processes.

12. Planning Performance Measurement & KPI
     This section defines the key performance indicators (KPIs) used to measure the effectiveness,
     stability, and discipline of the integrated planning and execution system. Performance measurement
     is intended to support continuous improvement, enhance planning quality, and strengthen execution
     reliability, rather than to assign individual blame

     12.1    Key Performance Indicators

     The following KPIs shall be used as primary indicators of planning and execution performance:

     a. Plan Attainment
        Definition:
        The percentage of planned activities that are successfully executed as scheduled within the
        approved plan.

          Purpose:
          To measure execution reliability and the organization’s ability to deliver committed work.

          Interpretation:
          • ≥ 90%         → Excellent (high execution reliability)
          • 85–89% → Acceptable
          • 75–84% → Marginal
          • < 75%         → Poor (high execution risk)

     b. Plan Stability
        Definition:
        The degree to which the approved plan remains unchanged after freeze (30D and 7D), reflecting
        the level of planning discipline and control.

          Purpose:
          To measure the effectiveness of planning, readiness validation, and adherence to freeze
          discipline.

          Key Indicators:
          • Number of changes after freeze
          • Frequency of re-sequencing or re-prioritization
          • Volume of deviations from approved plan

     c.   Break-in Frequency
          Definition:
          The number of break-in activities introduced after plan approval within a defined period.

          Purpose:
          To measure the level of planning disruption and effectiveness of early screening and readiness
          processes.
