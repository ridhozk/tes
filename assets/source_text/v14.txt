Introduction
Concept and Objectives of Integrated Planning 
The purpose of this document is to outline the processes and governance for Integrated Planning at Medco E&P Grissik Ltd (MEPG). Integrated Planning is a set of cross-functional processes designed to combine, sequence, and coordinate all activities across short-term (Operational), medium-term (Tactical), and long-term (Strategic) Horizons within the Medco Asset Plan1. 
The goal is to produce an approved and prioritized planning and scheduling process that maximizes stakeholder value by efficiently identifying critical activities, resolving conflicts, and managing opportunities. These processes are designed to minimize siloed thinking and promote a collaborative approach to planning.

Scope
The guidelines apply to the Integrated Planning and Scheduling (P&S) activities for MEPG Asset-operated PSCs, Exploration, and Development activities. It covers the integration and alignment of planning activities across the following planning horizons and processes:
Asset Development Plan (ADP);
Field Depletion Plan (FDP);
Long-Term Plan (LTP), which includes the 24-month and outer-years planning processes;
Short-term Planning, which includes 12-month, 90-day, 30-day and 7-day planning processes.
This guideline is meant to establish the planning principles, processes, and governance required to integrate activities across relevant MEPG functions and disciplines. Function-specific planning and execution practices shall remain governed by their respective applicable procedures and guidelines, while maintaining alignment with relevant scope of requirements established in this guideline.




Philosophy & Principles
Integrated Planning Philosophy
At MEPG, Integrated Planning uses a cross-functional and collaborative approach to align activities across three planning horizons: (1) Strategic, (2) Tactical, and (3) Operational. Activities are integrated and prioritized based on risk, business value, readiness, and available resources to support informed decision-making and effective execution.
Integrated Planning provides a common framework to align functions and identify constraints, opportunities, and ownership early in the planning process. The process aims to produce coordinated, resourced, prioritized, and approved plans and schedules, while continuously improving planning and execution performance.

Integrated Planning Strategy
Organization and Ownership. 
Planning process in MEPG shall be supported by competent planning resources at both the Asset and Functional levels. Each planning time horizon (strategic, tactical, and operational) shall have clearly defined ownership, responsibilities, and authority of approvals.

Goal Alignment
All MEPG Assets, Functions, and relevant stakeholders shall align their activities with agreed short, medium, and long-term agreed-upon business goals and Planning and Scheduling (P&S) objectives.
Integrated Planning and Scheduling.  
All activities within MEPG asset, including contractors, shall be planned, scheduled, and assessed in alignment with the Integrated Planning process.
Integration Across Planning Horizon
The Strategic, Tactical, and Operational horizons shall remained aligned. Any changes of plan, priorities, and decisions shall be communicated and assessed between horizon to maintain consistency from long-term planning to field execution.
Planning tools, visualization and communication.  
Planning tools, visualization and communication methods shall provide clear and consistent planning information. The methods use may be adjusted to meet stakeholders needs while maintaining a common view of the Planning & Scheduling (P&S) objectives.

Continuous Improvement.  
Regular and consistent reviews of planning performance shall be conducted to identify gaps, capture lesson learned, refine and maintain the relevancy of business processes.
Structured Planning Process.  
Each planning horizon shall adhere to a structured business process, which includes:
Integration of activities across different functions to ensure a unified approach.
Establishment of a baseline plan that accounts for key constraints, synergies, and potential conflicts for each time horizon.
Differentiation between activities requires high certainty and strict control from activities that can remain flexible for future adjustments.


Formal 
approval of the integrated plan by the designated ownership level for each planning horizon.
Feedback and alignment mechanisms between planning horizons to ensure changes in one horizon are reflected in the others where needed.

Regular review of planning performance to identify gaps, and opportunities for continuous improvement.


Function-Specific Practices
In addition to Integrated Planning Principles mentioned in section 2.2 that apply across MEPG, certain functions may apply tailored practices for P&S. These practices shall remain aligned with this guideline and function’s own procedures and standards.

Operations
Shutdown Planning.  Shutdown activities shall be coordinated with other functional activities and aligned with the maintenance schedules of both internal and external stakeholders, including buyers.
Projects 
Portfolio Management and Operability Execution.  The portfolio management of facilities and wells, from the pre-development through to execution, including contractor activities, shall follow the Integrated Planning process and comply with the applicable Medco Project Excellence Process (MPEP)
Operability Assurance (OA). Operability assurance shall support operations engagement in portfolio management, facilitating adequate input during project development. During execution, the Operations Readiness Review process will support operations teams in preparation for startup, ensuring seamless handover and project closeout phases.
Well Operations and Services
Procurement Planning. Procurement activities shall commence early during the strategic planning phase to account for possible and contingencies in the tactical period.
Well Delivery and Drilling Plan (WDDP). This processes shall be utilized to establish and regularly review baseline plans, with the objective of minimizing late-stage changes.
Commercial
Sales Agreement Alignment. Contractual events, rights, and obligations outlined in each sales agreement shall be integrated across relevant functions to optimize, protect, and/or create value for the company.
Market Alignment. Market drivers shall be regularly reviewed and reflected in production planning to to keep P&S aligned with commercial conditions.
Planning System & Horizon
MEPG Integrated Planning connects long-term Asset planning with short-term operational planning and field execution. The planning system consists of different planning horizons, with the level of detail, readiness, and planning certainty increasing as activities move closer to execution.
Each planning horizon is connected and should not be managed independently. Changes in Asset priorities, assumptions, constraints, or activity status may require review and alignment across related planning horizons. The planning horizons and their relationship are described in Section 3.1 below.
Horizon Breakdown
The MEPG Integrated Planning system covers the following planning processes and horizons:
Field Depletion Plan (FDP) 
The Field Depletion Plan (FDP) provided the integrated technical basis for field depletion and development planning. It is prepared at field level and integrated relevant subsurface, wells, facilities, operations, and other technical considerations.  The FDP process and approval requirements shall follow the applicable Medco Field Depletion Plan Guideline (Doc. No: GL-001/06.2021/MEDC/RESV-TA).
The FDP processes reviews field and reservoir performance, reserves and resources, production outlook, depletion and operational strategies, existing and future well activities, surveillance requirements, facility requirements, and technical feasibility. The resulting technical evaluation and identified opportunities provide inputs to the Asset Development Plan (ADP). 
Asset Development Plan (ADP) 
The Asset Development Plan (ADP) is created to establish the long-term development direction of the Asset. It builds on the FDP and integrates technical, commercial, economic, and business considerations at Block or Asset level. The ADP process and approval requirements shall follow the applicable Asset Development Plan Guideline (No. GL-002/06.2021/MEDC/RESV-TA).
The ADP processes reviews: 1) Asset scenario planning, 2) long-term growth 3) divestment planning, 4) end-of-life, 5) resource base & development options, 6) production profiles & DOE and 7) commercial considerations. The selected Asset strategy and development scenarios is then used to provide inputs to the Long-Term Plan (LTP) working horizon who then serves as a reflection of vision and mission of MEPG.


Long-Term Plan (LTP)
Long-Term Plan (LTP) translates the Asset strategy and ADP scenarios into an integrated long-term business plan, covering a ten-year outlook. It combines production, subsurface opportunities, development activities, operating assumptions, commercial considerations, manpower, and cost spending (CAPEX/OPEX) outlook. The LTP process and approval requirements shall follow the applicable Integrated Business Planning Guideline – E&P  (No. ID-G-BU-GG0-GUI-GN-00-3005).
This horizon is developed through the annual MEPI’s Integrated Business Planning cycle and updated in accordance with the applicable cycle set (Updated LTP) by E&P Planning division. For integrated planning purposes, LTP horizon consists of two distinct timelines:
24-Month Planning Period. This timeline period provides a more detailed short to medium-term activities and major milestones in assets. Planning within this period timeline requires early cross-functional alignments activities such as 1) shutdown, 2) major maintenance, 3) projects, 4) drilling, 5) well activities, 6) major procurement, and 7) key contractual or commercial requirements. 
Activities within this period are then reviewed for their timing, dependencies, constraint, interfaces, and alignment with the approved ADP plan. Relevant activities are then detailed progressively into the 12-month plan.
Outer-Years Period. This timeline period covers the remaining LTP planning horizon beyond the first 24 months. It also provides a longer-term view of Asset development, production, major spendings (CAPEX/OPEX), opportunities, and key business milestones.
Within this timeline period, the focus is more towards strategic direction, major assumptions, scenarios, and longer-term constraints. Relevant activities should be reviewed more thoroughly as they move closer to the 24-month planning period.
12-Month Plan (12M)
The 12-Month Plan (12M) is a rolling outlook of operationally impacting activities expected within the next 12 months. 12M provides early visibility of future activities and helped with the initial screening stage for short-term operational planning. 12M plan may include major operations, maintenance programs, planned shutdowns, projects, well activities, and other activities that may impact operational capacity or needing field execution.
Activities are reviewed at a high-level for priority, risk and mitigation plan, major constraint/blocker, interfaces, and resource requirements. After approval, 12M planned activities may progress into 90-Day Plan (90D) for further planning and prioritization.
90-Day Plan (90D)
The 90-Day Plan (90D) is a rolling outlook of activities anticipated within the next 90 days. It consolidates and prioritizes activities before detailed execution readiness validation. Activities are reviewed for priority, risk and mitigation plan, readiness, execution feasibility, resource and capacity requirements, SIMOPS, and cross-functional alignment.
Activities approved are Gate 1 during this stage become planned candidates and may proceed to detailed readiness validation for the 30-Day Plan. Gate 1 approval does not represent execution commitment.
30 Day plan (30D)
The 30-Day Plan (30D) is the execution commitment horizon. It is a rolling 30-day plan covering activities that have progressed through Gate 1 readiness review and are proposed for execution.
Activities are reviewed for the defined scope, manpower, materials, equipment, contracts, permits, HSE requirements, Management of Change (MOC), SIMOPS, resource availability, and other applicable execution constraints.
Activities that meet the applicable readiness requirements and receive Gate 2 approval are committed for execution and declared Frozen. Changes to committed activities shall follow the applicable break-in or change control process.
7 Day Plan/Plan Control (7D)
The 7-Day Plan is the detailed weekly execution control plan derived from the approved 30-Day Plan. It provides the activity sequencing and coordination required for day-to-day field execution. 7D planning process confirms final readiness, site coordination, manpower and resource deployment, SIMOPS, execution sequencing, and short-term constraints.
Gate 3 approval authorizes weekly execution of approved activities within the Frozen execution window. New scope, additional activities, or significant method changes shall not be introduced without the applicable break-in or change control process.
Activity-Based Planning Processes
In addition to planning horizons described in Section 3.1, MEPG applies activity-based planning processes for specific types of work. These processes are not separate planning horizons, but provides the function-specific planning, assurance, and readiness requirements needed for activities to be integrated into the applicable planning horizons.
Activities from these processes shall be aligned with the Integrated Planning & Scheduling (P&S) process based on their timing, operational impact, stakeholders, constraint, and execution requirements.
Maintenance Planning
This process covers the preparation, and coordination of preventive, corrective, and other maintenance activities required to maintain reliability, asset integrity, and operational performance of MEPG’s asset. The process includes: 1) work scope and work order preparation, 2) maintenance timing, 3) material and equipment requirements, 4) procurement and delivery status, 5) manpower and contractor requirements, and 6) operational constraints. Relevant maintenance activities shall be integrated into the applicable 12M, 90D, 30D, and 7D plans based on their criticality, readiness, and impact on operations.

Project Planning
This process covers the development, planning, and execution preparation of applicable facility and development projects. This process shall be performed in accordance with the applicable Medco Project Excellence Process (MPEP) (No. MPEP-PM-GUI-03) where applicable. The project process shall establish the required project scope, schedule, technical readiness, cost, contracting and procurement requirements, resources, risks, and key project milestones.
Project activities and milestones that affect production, Operations, shutdown windows, SIMOPS, field resources, logistics, or other functions shall be included in the applicable planning horizons. This guideline governs the coordination of those interfaces and execution requirements without replacing the applicable MPEP project governance. 
Shutdown Planning
This process covers the preparation, and coordination of planned activities requiring facility shutdown, production reduction, or significant operational intervention. This process shall integrate the work requirements of Operations, Maintenance, Projects, Wells, and other relevant functions. The planning process shall consider: 1) shutdown scope, 2) production impact (through LPO), 3) activity sequencing, 4) SIMOPS, 5) manpower and contractors, materials, 6) equipment, logistics, and 7) any applicable commercial or external stakeholder requirements.
Major shutdown and resource requirements shall be identified in the applicable LTP and 12M timeline periods. Individual shutdown activities shall be progressively detailed, inspected for activities, and scheduled through the 90D, 30D, and 7D planning horizons as execution approaches.
Early Operations Planning
This process covers Operations involvement during project development to ensure operability, reliability, maintainability, and operational requirements are considered before startup and handover. Early Operations involvement shall follow the applicable Operability Assurance (OA) requirements, including MPEP-PD-GUI-01 where applicable. Operations shall provide relevant input to operating requirements, maintenance needs, operational risks, and future readiness requirements during project development.
As the project approaches execution, startup, and handover, relevant Operations readiness activities shall be included in the applicable planning horizons. This includes, where applicable, manpower, operating procedures, training, maintenance requirements, materials, operational constraint, and startup support.

















Work Process/Flow
The Integrated Planning work process establishes a common flow for developing, reviewing, approving, and monitoring plans across the planning horizons described in Section 3.1. The process is iterative, allowing assumptions, priorities, constraints, and execution results to be reviewed and reflected in the relevant planning horizons.
Each planning horizon shall, at minimum, include the following process stages:
Initiation and Input. The planning process is initiated based on the applicable planning cycle, business requirement, operational need, or previous planning horizon output. Relevant functions shall identify and submit the required activities, assumptions, opportunities, and planning information.
Integration and Review. Planning inputs shall be consolidated and reviewed across relevant functions. The review shall consider priorities, risks, dependencies, constraints, resource requirements, readiness, and potential conflicts based on the level of detail required for the respective planning horizon.
Decision and Approval. The integrated plan shall be reviewed and approved by the designated Plan Owner or approval authority for the respective planning horizons. Activities that do not meet the applicable requirements shall be revised, deferred, or returned for further development.
Plan Hand-Off and Progression. Approved plans and relevant activities shall provide inputs to the next applicable planning horizon. The level of activity detail, readiness, and execution certainty shall progressively increase as activities move closer to execution.
Performance Monitoring and Control. Plan progress, changes, constraints, and performance shall be monitored by the responsible planning and functional teams. Significant deviations or changes shall be reviewed and managed through the applicable plan administration, change control, or break-in process.
Feedback and Adjustment. Planning and execution results, updated assumptions, and lessons learned shall be used to update the relevant planning horizon. Feedback may result in changes to activity timing, priorities, assumptions, or future to maintain alignment across planning horizons.
The planning flow is not strictly linear. Updated technical information, changes in business or operational priorities, emerging constraints, and execution results may require plans to be reviewed and adjusted at the relevant planning horizons.

Tools, System & References
Integrated Planning Processes in MEPG use various tools, systems, and corporate references to support its planning, data management, performance monitoring, activity coordination, and reporting across the planning horizons.
The tools and systems used may differ depending on the planning horizons, function, and type of activity. Where multiple systems are used, all stakeholders involved shall ensure that information remains consistent, current, and traceable across the relevant planning process.
The main tools, systems, and planning references used in MEPG’s Integrated Planning may include:
Integrated Business Planning (IBP) module in Medco BPM. Used to support business planning, budgeting, forecasting, and consolidation of planning information.
SAP. Used to support cost planning, cost allocation, budget monitoring, and tracking of expenditure against the applicable organizational and activity structure.
ARIS. Used to document and map applicable business processes, workflows, and process interfaces.
MCRS. Used to support applicable cost, resource, or management reporting requirements within the planning process.
SharePoint. Used as a common document and information-sharing platform for files, documents, and controlled planning information.
Well Delivery and Drilling Plan (WDDP). Provides the applicable planning framework and baseline planning reference for well delivery and drilling activities.
Medco Project Excellence Process (MPEP). Provides the applicable project governance, assurance, and planning requirements for projects across the relevant project lifecycle.
Production Forecasting Tools/Gas Processing Tools (GPT). Applicable forecasting tools and models may be used to support Short-Term Production Forecasting (STPF), production outlook, and planning assumptions.
Other Applicable Tools and Systems. Other function-specific tools, systems, templates, or planning references may be used where required to support the applicable planning and execution process.

Roles, Approvals & Governance
Plan Ownership and Approval
Each planning horizon, plan, program, or any grouped set of activities shall have defined planning ownership and approval authority appropriate to its scope and level of decision-making.
The Plan Owner is accountable for the planning outcome and progression of the plan within the applicable planning horizon. Plan ownership may reside with Subsurface, Asset Management, E&P Planning, Operations, or other designated functional leadership, depending on the nature and scope of the plan.

Supporting functions, including Projects, Well Operations, Engineering, Commercial, Relations, HSE, and other relevant functions, shall provide the required planning inputs and support the delivery of their respective activities.
In general, the Plan Owner is accountable for:
planning performance and delivery of the agreed work program;
key planning assumptions, premises, and material changes;
resolution or escalation of conflicts, constraints, issues, and opportunities;
milestone and plan status updates; and
review of resource requirements and constraints applicable to the plan.
Each planning horizon shall establish the following governance roles, as applicable:
Plan Owner. Accountable for the overall planning outcome, key planning decisions, and progression of the plan.
Planning Process Focal Point. Coordinates the applicable planning cycle, planning inputs, review forum, hand-offs, and implementation of the planning process.
Planning Administrator. Consolidates, updates, and maintains the required planning information and supports alignment of cross-functional activity schedules.
Approval Authority. Reviews and authorizes the plan or planning decision within the assigned level of authority.
The Planning Process Focal Point facilitates the planning process but does not replace the accountability of the Plan Owner or individual activity owners. Similarly, the Planning Administrator supports plan development and control but remains dependent on timely and accurate inputs from the responsible functions and activity owners.
Approval shall be performed at the appropriate organizational level for each planning horizon. As plans progress toward execution, the approval focus shifts from technical and strategic direction to prioritization, execution commitment, and field execution control.
Approval of an integrated plan does not transfer accountability for individual activities. The respective activity or work owners remain accountable for the completeness, readiness, progress updates, and delivery of their assigned scope.

Activities shall be included in the Integrated Planning process based on the level of visibility required within each planning horizon. The level of inclusion may vary depending on the activity type, operational or production impact, resource requirements, business materiality, cross-functional stakeholders, and other applicable planning considerations.
Inclusion Criteria Matrix
Not every activity requires the same level of visibility across all planning horizons. This guideline provides general criteria for determining which activities should be included in each horizon, considering the following factors:
Activity Type. Activities are identified as either Function-specific or Integrated, depending on the level of cross-functional coordination required.
Planning Horizon. Activity inclusion is assessed across the applicable planning horizons, from LTP through short-term operational planning and execution control.
Inclusion Criteria. Activities may be included based on defined conditions or thresholds, such as operational or production impact, workforce or resource requirements, financial materiality, regulatory or approval requirements, and other activity-specific considerations.
Schedule Visualization Granularity (level-of-detail). The level of schedule visualization generally increases as activities progress toward execution.
Where “ALL” is stated in the matrix, all activities within the applicable category shall be included. Where a specific threshold or condition is stated, the activity shall be included when the applicable criterion is met.
The criteria represent the minimum level of visibility required within the Integrated Planning process. Functions may maintain more detailed plans and schedules within their respective planning processes and systems. Activities below the stated criteria may also be included where required due to risk, operational impact, cross-functional dependency, resource constraints, or management direction.

Managing the Plans
The management of integrated plans requires consistent processes for developing, reviewing, updating, and controlling activities across the applicable planning horizons. As activities progress from more strategic, longer-term planning toward tactical and executional, the level of detail, certainty, readiness, and control shall progressively increase.
Plans shall be managed to maintain alignment across functions and planning horizons, identify and resolve constraints and conflicts, confirm activity readiness, and control changes to approved plans. The requirements in this section apply according to the purpose and level of detail of each planning horizon.
Acceptance/Input Criteria 
Maintaining consistent acceptance and input criteria is crucial for ensuring the compatibility and coordination of activities from various sources into a unified integrated plan. This approach facilitates the identification of potential conflicts, such as skilled labor availability, rig constraints, and capital limitations, while also uncovering synergies and optimizing the sequencing of activities.
Activities shall meet the applicable inclusion criteria for the relevant planning horizon as defined in Appendix C – Planning Activity Inclusion Criteria. The information required to support planning, integration, and review shall be progressively developed as activities move toward execution. Typical input criteria for each planning horizon are detailed in Appendix D.
12 Month Plan (12M)
12M Plan provides a rolling view of operationally impacting activities expected within the next twelve months. It serves as the transition between the LTP and detailed operational planning by consolidating relevant activities from the approved LTP and other applicable functional or operational inputs.
12M Plan is intended for early screening, integration, and coordination. Activities included in the plan are not yet committed for execution. The plan provides sufficient visibility to identify potential priorities, dependencies, interfaces, risks, constraints, and resource requirements before activities progress into the 90D Plan.
Activities proposed for inclusion in the 12M Plan shall, as applicable, include:
activity identification and description;
responsible function and activity owner;
indicative timing and major milestones;
preliminary scope and duration;
major resource and capacity requirements;
key dependencies and interfaces;
known risks and constraints;
potential operational, production, HSE, SIMOPS, or commercial impacts; and
other information required to support cross-functional screening and integration.
12M plan shall be periodically reviewed and updated to reflect changes in priorities, assumptions, activity timing, constraints, and the latest available information. Activities approaching the operational planning window shall be progressively developed to the level of information and readiness required for consideration in the 90-Day Plan.
Progression from the 12M Plan into the 90D Plan shall not constitute execution commitment. Activities shall remain subject to the applicable prioritization, readiness, and approval requirements before progressing toward the 30D Frozen Plan and subsequent execution.

Readiness Gate Review: This review assesses whether an activity is prepared for integration into the execution phase. It applies specifically to activities scheduled within the 30- and 90-day planning horizons. Activities failing to meet readiness criteria will not proceed to execution.

Managing Constraints
When developing plans, especially at the 30/90-Day Plan level, several constraints must be considered. These constraints are typically classified into two categories: static and dynamic. Static Constraints tend to remain relatively unchanged over time and include factors such as the number of beds, life boat seat availability, regulatory permits, personnel access to specific areas, ongoing SIMOPS (Simultaneous Operations) restrictions, and limitations set by design or building codes.
Dynamic Constraints are subject to frequent changes and require a more responsive management process. Examples include weather conditions, seasonal access, availability of specialist services and personnel, and dynamic SIMOPS conditions (e.g., ongoing work, equipment downtime, etc.).
When developing plans, it is essential that all identified constraints are understood, communicated, and updated regularly. The following are typical constraints that should be considered as part of the P&S process.
Permitting and Other Stakeholders Approvals
The readiness of a work activity should consider all necessary clearances, permits, and approvals from relevant stakeholders. This includes budget and AFE approvals from government/SKKMigas, Partners, and internal Medco, as well as permits from agencies such as MIGAS, Seacomm, Aircomm, and local governments.
Resources Planning & Manpower Histogram
Resources, including headcount and associated costs, are tied to planned activities. Headcount resources should be validated and leveled according to numbers, competencies, and timing (e.g., manpower histogram). Hiring plans should also be considered as a potential constraint to ensure that the required skills are available when needed.
Cost constraints should be validated against the budgeted items in the LTP and the approved AFEs.
Personnel on Board and Bedding Capacity
One of the primary constraints to consider when preparing the 30/90-Day Field Plan is the Personnel on Board (POB) capacity at field locations. The total manpower should not exceed the pre-defined maximum POB.
Crew Change Schedule & Logistics
Field Asset Operations provide regular transportation for personnel mobilization and demobilization. Mobilization outside of these scheduled times may require additional approvals, such as from the area Superintendent.
Material transportation from Jakarta to the field is arranged regularly by the Material Management group. Contractors should also consider transportation arrangements for goods and materials when developing the plan.
General Contractors Capacity
The availability of 3rd party resources should be confirmed before they are included in the 30/90-Day Plan. The contractor's schedule must reflect their readiness to execute the work.
Material Readiness and Supply Chain 
Materials and contracts required for any task should be identified early, considering the procurement/contracting process, delivery times, and transportation to the field. Task leads are responsible for identifying the required materials, quantities, and specifications, and passing this information to the planners to create requisitions in the system if the materials are not available in stock.
If materials are available in stock, maintenance planners should make reservations and communicate with the Materials Management team to ensure the materials are available when needed at the site.
Planners will facilitate communication with Procurement and Materials Management through the contract administrator based in the Jakarta Office to expedite the procurement process, materials delivery, and report on materials readiness status.
Activities included in the 90-Day Plan must have confirmed materials delivery prior to the scheduled execution. Activities listed in the 30-Day Plan should have all materials delivered to the site or be en route. Any exceptions require approval from the OIM or Field Manager.
Work Pack 
The 90-Day Plan includes all activities that meet the 'entry criteria' within the 90-day window. Activities listed on the 90-Day Plan (but not yet on the 30-Day Plan) have at least 30 days to develop the work pack before being included in the 30-Day Plan.
Operations and Maintenance Activities
BPM identifies all plant and equipment that require maintenance across all assets and specifies when and by whom the work will be carried out. This includes Planned Maintenance Routines (PM), which are recurring activities performed at a set frequency, and Corrective Maintenance Routines (CM), which are created to address additional work arising from inspections, breakdowns, or repairs to plant and equipment.
Although BPM identifies when Preventive Maintenance activities are due, certain activities may need to be included in the Integrated Plan to raise awareness among the maintenance crew and field supervisors, ensuring alignment and optimizing resources. 
Risk Assessment
The characterization of risks associated with planned activities can facilitate prioritization. Risks should be evaluated in terms of potential impacts on safety, the environment, execution, production, and other relevant areas. Risk assessment and communication should be integrated throughout the planning process, with particular emphasis at the 30/90 Day field execution planning stage.
Strategic Planning Horizon
At the strategic level, risk assessments will be conducted based on their potential long-term impact on MEPG. Business prioritization at this level may encompass safety, environmental impact, production, and high-level SIMOPS activities. Specifically, completion risk for Major Projects can be characterized by developing risked schedules using a probabilistic Contingency Breakdown Report (CBR) methodology. This methodology may include calculating P10, P50, and P90 schedule and cost estimates.
Tactical Planning Horizon
At the Tactical Level, risk evaluations, similar to those at the Strategic Horizon, will address high-level risks but with a focus on individual program or asset risks. For instance, key risks to Major Projects will be identified in terms of both cost and schedule impacts. For operating assets, potential production downtime due to shutdown windows will be considered, and significant upcoming maintenance programs will be highlighted.
Operational Planning Horizon
At the operational level, detailed risk assessments are conducted for planned activities, considering the inherent hazards of each activity, project, or operation. 
During the design and planning stages, these assessments include analyses such as Hazard Identification (HAZID), Hazard and Operability (HAZOP) studies, SIMOPS analysis, Safety Integrity Level (SIL) evaluations, Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA)/CBR, and other design risk assessments. As activities transition to field execution, the focus shifts to safety and execution readiness, using tools such as Pre-Job Hazard Analyses (PJHA) and toolbox talks. The MEPG risk matrix (Appendix E) may be used as applicable.
Contingency Planning
The outcomes of risk assessments will guide the evaluation, development, and planning of contingency measures. Key features of contingency management are as follows:
Contingency plans are developed by cross-functional teams representing relevant areas of expertise.
The allocation of resources and the approval process are commensurate with the assessed level of risk.
Contingencies for tasks and equipment, including necessary materials, resources, and logistics, are integrated into the project plan and schedule.
Schedule contingencies incorporate a defined recovery strategy.
Contingency planning may utilize a formalized methodology, such as the QRA/CBR employed by Projects, or be addressed as a standard component of regular planning meetings.

Change Control for Operational Planning Horizon
Changes to the approved 30-day plan should be minimized. However, if a change is deemed necessary, it must follow the appropriate authorization process.
For field operation activities, changes that do not affect constraints (e.g., bed capacity, SIMOPS, cost, HSE risks, or other issues) can be approved by the Plan Owner. If the proposed change impacts constraints, it must be escalated to the OIM for review, with final approval at the discretion of the Field Manager (FM).
When evaluating changes that impact constraints, the FM should consider factors such as the criticality of the work, resource availability, logistics, POB capacity, and other relevant considerations.
A ‘change’ to the approved 30-Day Plan is defined as any of the following:
Adding work to the approved plan, particularly if it requires additional resources or support. Depending on the nature and criticality of the work, email confirmation from the FM or OIM may suffice.
Delays to tasks within the approved plan that could impact constraints or other tasks in the plan.
Inclusion of projects or activities that require additional budget.
Other additions, cancellations, or delays deemed substantial enough to require authorization.

Assessing Performance
Debriefing and After Action Reviews (AAR)
The AAR is a valuable tool for learning from both successes and failures. Its primary objective is to engage participants involved in the activity to review variances from the plan. This collaborative approach allows leaders to leverage group dynamics and reach a shared understanding of key lessons learned.
The AAR should focus on identifying lessons that can significantly enhance performance. These insights, whether they are best practices or opportunities for improvement, should be integrated into the overall strategy to drive continual progress.
The benefits of the AAR approach:
Provides valuable insights for others involved in similar activities, helping to avoid repetitive mistakes and unnecessary relearning.
Facilitates a common understanding of actual events and variances from the original plan.
Identifies opportunities for improvement and establishes actionable steps for progress.
Ultimately aims to improve the way we conduct our business and achieve better outcomes.
Further details about the AAR, including its processes, structure, and application, can be found in Appendix G – Conducting After Action Review.
Planning Compliance 
Assessing compliance with the BU planning processes requires multilateral communication between planners, plan users, and plan owners. Compliance is not just about whether processes are being followed, but also about their understanding, value, and their role in facilitating continuous improvement. If processes are not being adhered to, it may indicate that the processes themselves need to be rectified.

Shutdown plans that cause a reduction of at least 50% in the normal production rate.

Business Performance Review 
Robust management systems are designed to drive continuous improvement in organizational effectiveness and efficiency. Three fundamental components contribute significantly to sustained business performance improvement:

Governance: The framework that governs and sustains business performance reviews over time.
Documentation: The systematic collection of records that define the underlying data, analyses, and visualizations.
Metrics:  The established standards used to measure business performance. Performance reviews for P&S typically consider the following metrics:
Performance in terms of milestones, production, and costs across various timeframes (e.g., plan-on-plan, gate-on-gate, year-on-year).
KPIs, including man-hour, cost, and schedule efficiency, and plan attainment.
Clear accountabilities for plan ownership, review, and business performance have been established within the business processes, with a focus on the Tactical and Strategic Horizons.
Key Performance Indicator (KPI)
KPIs are metrics that are visible across the organization and are used to highlight and drive performance within P&S. These key metrics are documented in the business process maps associated with each horizon and in the overall Management Control and Reporting System (MCRS).
At the Operational Horizon, which focuses on the short term, metrics related to attainment, stability, and efficiency are applied. In contrast, longer-term KPIs primarily assess compliance with the planning process. Certain functions, such as Projects, maintain supplementary statistics for internal purposes, including man-hour, cost, and schedule efficiency; however, these are not comprehensively detailed within the BU procedure.
The following secondary performance measures may be considered for further development into defined KPIs:
Plan Utilization: This metric measures the extent to which planned activities on baselined work align with the resources available for their completion.
Plan Stability: This metric quantifies the number of interventions or changes made to the approved plan during the defined planning period.
Work Efficiency: This metric compares the amount of planned work performed (both scheduled and unscheduled) to the resources available for performing that work.
Disturbance/Delay: This metric tracks non-productive time associated with activity execution, categorized using standardized reporting codes, such as delays due to material availability, weather conditions, or permit approvals.

Appendix F – Activity Change Request Process

Reason for Plan Change
Changes to the 30-day plan must be approved by the Plan Owner due to the potential risks associated with adding or modifying activities. Such changes, if not carefully managed, could lead to Health, Safety, and Environmental (HSE) incidents, failure to meet forecasted deadlines, or negative impacts on overall plan performance.
There are three types of change to the plan:
Addition of New Activities
Deletion of Existing Activities
Modifications to Ongoing Activities within the 30-Day Plan
For operations activities, all proposed changes must be reviewed and approved during the daily or weekly meetings with the Superintendent, OIM, and/or Offsite Managers. These changes must also be documented through email confirmation or meeting minutes. Alignment with the existing schedule and required resources must be addressed during the meeting.
Any proposed changes that may impact constraints—such as safety, operational resources, logistics support, production, or budget—must be initiated through a Plan Change Request. At a minimum, such changes require approval from the Plan Owner.
New Activity to Plan
This process ensures that all unplanned activities with potential impacts on constraints (such as accommodations, operational resources, logistics support), production, or budget are clearly defined and agreed upon before being incorporated into the ongoing 30-day plans.
Changes with potential impacts on constraints—such as safety, operational resources, logistics support, production, or budget—require an approved Plan Change Request (PCR) form, with the following process:
Submission of PCR Form:
The Activity Sponsor completes and submits the PCR form to the Asset Planner for evaluation.
If there are no significant constraints (e.g., accommodations or SIMOPS issues), the changes can be forwarded directly to the Plan Owner for approval.
If there is a major impact on constraints, the matter must be escalated to the OIM for resolution. Depending on the complexity of the change, the OIM may convene a meeting with the Field Manager to discuss and resolve the issue.
The Field Manager reviews the PCR form, considering the priority, justification, and potential impacts on the current plan. Based on this review, the Field Manager decides whether to approve or reject the proposed activity.
The Asset Planner informs the Activity Sponsor whether the proposed activity is approved or rejected, including the reasons for the decision. If the change impacts other activities, the owners of those activities must be informed of the implications, and adjustments or reprioritization may be required.
Deletion of Activities
The deletion of an activity can be decided by the OIM after evaluating the following factors:
Impact on Budget and Production Opportunity: The potential consequences of continuing the activity versus its removal, including financial and production considerations.
Resource Reallocation: The reallocation of resources freed by the deletion and how it affects other operations.
Impact on Other Activities: Any effects on other activities that depend on or follow sequentially from the deleted activity.
Approval for deleting an activity that may impact constraints or resources must be documented through a PCR. The OIM is responsible for providing justification based on the considerations outlined above, while the Asset Planner is responsible for recording and documenting the justification.

Changes within ongoing 30 day planning
Any change to a plan that extends beyond three days from its original 30-day schedule requires alignment with the existing schedule and available resources. If the change impacts other activities, the respective activity owners must be informed, as they may need to re-prioritize their work accordingly.

Appendix G – Conducting After Action Review

Planning the AAR
Identify the activity to be reviewed.
Decide on the timing of the AAR – it should be conducted as soon as possible after the event.
Coordinate with the leader to determine who needs to attend.
Ensure a suitable location or room is available.
The leader should encourage the organization to capture key lessons in preparation for the AAR

Preparing the AAR
Select a facilitator.
Confirm the date, time, location, and duration of the session in advance.
Set expectations for attendees to come prepared and ready to focus on key issues

Conducting the AAR
The AAR should be focused, facilitated, and last no longer than 60 minutes.
Typically, the AAR will involve input from multiple perspectives and will not be conducted in isolation.
Promote active participation through group discussion.
Facilitate discussions in chronological order of events.
Record key lessons on a flip chart or similar tool.
Clearly define and agree on action items, including the responsible parties, specific tasks, and deadlines.
Highlight the potential impact of each lesson where known.

Follow up the AAR
Ensure documented lessons are tracked to closure, with their added value quantified. Use an Action Register to monitor progress.
Provide the leader with access to the AAR for discussion during lessons learned meetings.
